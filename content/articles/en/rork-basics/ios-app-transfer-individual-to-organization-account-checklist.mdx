I was picking an in-app purchase product ID for a new app one evening when I stopped typing. Should this ID live in my individual account, when I might set up a company later?

The New Subscription form in App Store Connect sat open, cursor blinking. A product ID is hard to change once it's in use. And yet I had been treating the account it lives in as something to "figure out someday."

The short answer is that there are **two** ways to move from individual to organization. One is asking Apple to update the membership itself from individual to organization. The other is creating a separate organization account and moving each app there with App Transfer. Which one you take changes how much you have to clear and how long you wait.

An app built in Rork ends up in your own Apple account once it's published, so this question reaches you sooner or later. What follows comes from opening Apple's pages one by one and reading them in November 2026.

## The two paths, side by side

An earlier version of this article said I'd found no official way to switch an account from individual to organization. That was wrong, and I'm sorry for it. Apple's [page on updating account information](https://developer.apple.com/help/account/manage-your-team/update-your-account-information/) has a section on updating an individual membership to an organization membership.

In summary, Apple asks that you:

- be the founder or co-founder of the organization
- provide details such as the organization's D-U-N-S Number
- be ready to submit business documents if asked, so Apple can verify the organization

App Transfer, on the other hand, moves apps one at a time into an organization account that already exists or that you create for the purpose.

The membership update looks lighter, and for someone with several apps, skipping the per-feature work is a real saving.

There are things I couldn't confirm, though. Apple's page doesn't say whether your Team ID stays the same after the switch. The same page also says that changing an organization name updates the vendor name on all your apps and resets identifierForVendor (IDFV) for existing users on their next update. I could not confirm whether an individual-to-organization update does the same. If your ad measurement relies on IDFV, I'd ask Apple before submitting.

One more thing: the Apple Developer Forums have quite a few threads about individual-to-organization requests that stayed pending for weeks or months. That isn't an official estimate, but I wouldn't plan on selling under the company name the week after you apply.

## How I choose

The rule I use fits in one sentence.

**If you're the founder and the same person keeps running the same apps, request the membership update. If the owner of the app is actually changing, use App Transfer.**

The practical side of handing an app to someone else is in [the day you let an app go](/articles/rork-business/rork-app-store-app-transfer-handover-checklist). Here I stay with the case where your own business changes shape.

## Conditions when you take App Transfer

From here on, the App Transfer path. These are the ones people hit first, from [Apple's app transfer criteria](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/transfer-an-app/app-transfer-criteria).

<tr><td>The app isn't Processing for Distribution, Waiting for Review, In Review, Accepted, Pending Developer Release or Pending Apple Release</td><td>Starting right after submitting an update</td></tr>
<tr><td>The app isn't on pre-order anywhere</td><td>A pre-order was opened early</td></tr>
<tr><td>In-app purchases are Approved, Ready to Submit, Developer Removed from Sale or Rejected</td><td>A new product is still out for review</td></tr>
<tr><td>No in-app purchase product ID matches one on any app in the recipient's account</td><td>A test app on the receiving side reuses the same ID</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

The first row matters if you mix the two paths. While a membership update request is in progress, your account is very likely in a "changing" state, and App Transfer's conditions can't be met. **Don't run both paths at once** — if I could leave only one note from this research, it would be that.

The last row was the one that made my stomach drop. Treat product IDs as something to tidy later, and the transfer stops right there.

## Write down the clearing work per app

[Apple's transfer overview](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/transfer-an-app/overview-of-app-transfer/) lists notes per feature. These are the ones that create real work for a small app with purchases, notifications and sign-in.

**1. TestFlight.** Turn TestFlight off for every beta version before you begin. Remove builds and testers, and clear Test Information in every localization.

**2. The shared secret for auto-renewable subscriptions.** The app must use an app-specific shared secret. Generate it before you initiate and give it to the recipient, who switches their server-side verification to it. After the transfer, the recipient generates a new one.

**3. Sign in with Apple.** You need a transfer identifier for every user. If the app is grouped with others, ungroup it first. The associated Service ID moves too, so remove that association beforehand if you want to keep it.

**4. Push notifications and keychain sharing.** The recipient's APNs certificates stay valid until they expire; after that a new certificate or key is needed. Keychain sharing keeps working only until the app is updated, after which users sign in once more.

**5. iCloud (CloudKit) and Apple Pay.** The CloudKit container moves to the recipient. If another app shares it, that app loses read and write access. The Apple Pay merchant ID doesn't move; the recipient creates a new one before the next update.

Apple also notes that Xcode Cloud data must be removed first, and that App Groups can be re-registered on the receiving side afterwards.

With two or more apps, I can no longer keep track of what to clear where. So I list it with a small script. You only flip the features to true while looking at App Store Connect.

```js
// transfer-prep.mjs — list what to clear before App Transfer, and the 60-day deadlines
// Usage: node transfer-prep.mjs <request date> [recipient accept date]   e.g. node transfer-prep.mjs 2026-12-01 2026-12-03
// Dates are calendar days, pinned to 00:00 UTC so a time zone offset can't shift them
const day = (s) => new Date(`${s}T00:00:00Z`);
const requested = day(process.argv[2] ?? "2026-12-01");
const accepted = process.argv[3] ? day(process.argv[3]) : null;

// Set true for each feature the app uses (fill this in with App Store Connect open)
const apps = [
  { name: "WallpaperApp", testflight: true, subscription: true, siwa: false, push: true, cloudkit: false, applePay: false },
  { name: "NoteApp",      testflight: false, subscription: false, siwa: true, push: false, cloudkit: true, applePay: false },
];

const tasks = {
  testflight:   "Remove TestFlight builds and testers; clear Test Information in every localization",
  subscription: "Generate an app-specific shared secret and hand it over (recipient regenerates after)",
  siwa:         "Generate a transfer_sub for every user (ungroup the app first if grouped)",
  push:         "Prepare an APNs key on the recipient team and swap it on your push server",
  cloudkit:     "Check whether another app shares the same CloudKit container",
  applePay:     "Recipient creates a new merchant ID before the next update",
};

const addDays = (d, n) => new Date(d.getTime() + n * 86400000).toISOString().slice(0, 10);

for (const app of apps) {
  const todo = Object.keys(tasks).filter((k) => app[k]).map((k) => `  - ${tasks[k]}`);
  console.log(`${app.name}: ${todo.length} item(s) to clear`);
  console.log(todo.length ? todo.join("\n") : "  - none (just back up)");
  if (app.siwa && accepted) {
    console.log(`  * Deadline to exchange transfer_sub for new IDs: ${addDays(accepted, 60)} (60 days after acceptance)`);
  }
}
console.log(`Requested ${addDays(requested, 0)} -> recipient must accept by ${addDays(requested, 60)} (60 days)`);
```

Running `node transfer-prep.mjs 2026-12-01 2026-12-03` on Node.js 22 prints:

```text
WallpaperApp: 3 item(s) to clear
  - Remove TestFlight builds and testers; clear Test Information in every localization
  - Generate an app-specific shared secret and hand it over (recipient regenerates after)
  - Prepare an APNs key on the recipient team and swap it on your push server
NoteApp: 2 item(s) to clear
  - Generate a transfer_sub for every user (ungroup the app first if grouped)
  - Check whether another app shares the same CloudKit container
  * Deadline to exchange transfer_sub for new IDs: 2027-02-01 (60 days after acceptance)
Requested 2026-12-01 -> recipient must accept by 2027-01-30 (60 days)
```

The dates are pinned to midnight UTC for a reason. My first version built them at midnight Japan time, and because `toISOString()` converts to UTC, every date came out one day early. Being off by a day is exactly the mistake you don't want in a deadline.

## If the app uses Sign in with Apple

For apps with Sign in with Apple, this is the heaviest part. According to [Apple's documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/signinwithapple/transferring-your-apps-and-users-to-another-team), the flow is:

1. The sending team gets an access token with client credentials (`grant_type=client_credentials`, `scope=user.migration`).
2. For each user, it posts the user's `sub` and the recipient's Team ID (`target`) to `https://appleid.apple.com/auth/usermigrationinfo` and receives a `transfer_sub`.
3. The data handed over leaves out your team-scoped user ID and the private relay email, identifying each person only by `transfer_sub`.
4. The recipient exchanges each `transfer_sub` for its own team-scoped ID within 60 days of accepting the transfer.

The request shape in Apple's documentation looks like this (I haven't run it in my own setup):

```http
POST /auth/usermigrationinfo HTTP/1.1
Host: appleid.apple.com
Content-Type: application/x-www-form-urlencoded
Authorization: Bearer {access_token}

sub={sub}&target={recipient_team_id}&client_id={client_id}&client_secret={client_secret}
```

The response is a single field, `{"transfer_sub":"..."}`. The sending team can generate these any time before acceptance and up to 60 days after it.

The easy one to miss: users who don't use any of your other apps can no longer be reached through their private relay address after the transfer. If you need to tell them anything, send it before you move.

While a transfer is pending, the sending side can't edit metadata, pricing, availability or in-app purchases. If you have a sale or price change planned that month, move the request date.

## Common misunderstandings

**"You can't switch from individual to organization."** — As above, Apple has an official update request. I repeated this one myself in the earlier version.

**"A transfer wipes reviews and changes the Bundle ID."** — Apple's overview says reviews and ratings stay and the Bundle ID is kept. The app can stay on sale during the move, and users keep getting updates.

**"You can enroll an organization under a trade name."** — The [enrollment page](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/) says DBAs, fictitious business names, trade names and branches aren't accepted. You need a legal entity that can sign contracts, a D-U-N-S Number, a work email on the organization's domain, and a public, working website. With an individual enrollment, your legal name appears as the seller.

**"Once accepted, it's done the same day."** — The recipient has 60 days from the request to accept, and processing takes up to two business days after. With Sign in with Apple, another 60-day exchange window follows.

## What carries over, and what doesn't

Apple says reviews, ratings and the Bundle ID carry over.

The Bundle ID can't be changed once a build has been uploaded. I wrote about that in [why to fix your app ID before the first release](/articles/rork-basics/rork-app-id-immutable-before-first-release); transfer is the sequel.

What doesn't carry over includes the Apple Pay merchant ID, Game Center matchmaking configuration and Wallet pass identifiers (passes need reissuing). For an app with only purchases and sign-in, the weight falls on the shared secret and Sign in with Apple.

## The next thing you'll hit

Right after the transfer, it's signing. Apple's acceptance page says to create new provisioning profiles in the recipient's developer account, tied to the transferred app's App ID and distribution certificate. If you build with Expo's EAS, the Apple team behind your credentials changes too, so recreate them on the receiving team before the next build.

The other is wording that follows the seller name: the operator name in your privacy policy and any legally required seller disclosures in the app or on your site. For Japan's specified commercial transactions notice, I wrote up [how I decide the address and phone number](/articles/rork-business/tokushoho-notation-before-paid-plan-address-phone-decision). The enrollment steps themselves are in [the Apple-side steps you do yourself](/articles/rork-basics/rork-apple-developer-account-steps-rork-cannot-automate).

## The line I drew that evening

At first I had put the account type and the product IDs in the same box marked "sort out later." Looking again, one of them has a way back and the other barely does.

**The account can change shape later; the IDs you've baked into an app mostly can't.** So the one thing I fix up front is an ID that would still make sense in whichever account the app ends up in. That's how I name new products now.

## One thing to do next

Open App Store Connect and write down, for each app, which of these five it uses: TestFlight, purchases, Sign in with Apple, push notifications, iCloud. Filling in the true values in the script above is enough.

Looking back, I stopped typing that evening because this list only existed in my head. The emptier it is, the lighter moving day will be — whichever path you take.
