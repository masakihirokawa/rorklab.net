Welcome back to the Friday weekly roundup. This week the standout articles on Rork Lab fell into two clusters: Rork Max compared against alternative tools, and long-horizon operational records — three months of one thing, one month side-by-side with another. The audience continues to reward sustained, real-world testing over feature introductions.

I'm Masaki Hirokawa, an artist and indie app developer shipping apps full-time since 2014 (about 50 million cumulative downloads). I use Rork for new-app prototyping and for adding features to existing apps in production. The five picks below all come straight from that work.

## #1 — Choosing Rork Max Over FlutterFlow and Replit, From an App-Business Perspective

[Choosing Rork Max over FlutterFlow and Replit — selection criteria from an indie app-business perspective](/articles/rork-ai/rork-max-vs-flutterflow-replit-indie-app-business-perspective)

The selection criteria I applied when putting Rork Max, FlutterFlow, and Replit side-by-side from a business owner's viewpoint. The deciding factors that landed me on Rork Max: real native code that remains in the project, and the freedom to go deep into native StoreKit / AdMob territory.

The article includes a table mapping each tool to the project scale, team size, and revenue model it tends to suit best. If you're early in your Rork evaluation, this is the kind of practical decision-making reference worth bookmarking.

## #2 — Three Months of Delegating App Store Review Replies to Claude in Chrome

[Three months of delegating App Store review replies to Claude in Chrome](/articles/rork-business/claude-in-chrome-app-store-review-reply-3months)

Three months of letting Claude in Chrome handle App Store and Google Play review replies across multiple apps, including the ones I shipped with Rork. Once you cross 30 replies a month, doing them all manually stops being viable; the article describes how I progressively expanded the AI's scope.

It includes the prompt template I converged on and the policy for which review types still require human review (bug reports, repeated 1-star). If you're exploring an "AI drafts, human approves" pattern for support, this is a concrete blueprint.

```text
# Reply prompt template (excerpt)
You are user support for App X.
For the following review body, lead with appreciation,
provide concrete next-step instructions in 3 lines or fewer,
and close with one sentence welcoming further feedback.
If you judge it to be a bug report, prefix the reply with the label [BUG REPORT].
```

## #3 — One Month Running Rork and Claude on Xcode Side-by-Side

[One month running Rork and Claude on Xcode side-by-side — the split that worked for wallpaper-app updates](/articles/rork-ai/rork-claude-on-xcode-side-by-side-month-wallpaper-app)

One month of using Rork for new prototypes and Claude on Xcode for modifications to existing production code. The article includes both "where Rork failed" and "where Claude on Xcode failed" with reproducible conditions.

The most valuable lesson: pasting Rork-generated SwiftUI into a mature production codebase often crashes because the state-management idioms differ. Routing Rork output through Claude on Xcode for a refactor pass before integration is what finally made the flow stable.

## #4 — Two Months After Switching to "Don't Check AdMob Dashboard Daily"

[Two months after switching to "don't check AdMob daily" — a four-tier Slack notification design for Rork apps](/articles/rork-business/rork-admob-not-checking-daily-slack-4-tier-notifications)

Two months after replacing daily AdMob dashboard checks with a four-tier Slack notification system (green: routine variation, yellow: caution, orange: needs review, red: urgent). The article walks through how to set the thresholds for each tier and how to filter out false positives.

Dashboard time dropped from "30 min/day" to "60 min/week", which is meaningful free time back. Especially impactful if you operate multiple apps in parallel.

## #5 — Three Weeks of Design Notes From Retrofitting iPad Support Into a Rork App

[Three weeks of design notes from retrofitting iPad support into a Rork-generated app](/articles/rork-dev/rork-ipad-retrofit-three-weeks-design-notes)

Rork generates with iPhone in mind, so running the result on iPad surfaces a cluster of issues at once — navigation that's still iPhone-shaped, fonts that are too small, Split View ignored. Three weeks of notes on cleaning that up.

The article walks through patterns for branching screen structure on SwiftUI's `horizontalSizeClass` and what specifically you need to address when migrating to `NavigationSplitView`. Useful reading before you start your own iPad retrofit.

## What This Week's Picks Have in Common

Two themes ran through this week: "how does Rork Max compare against other no-code / generative tools now that it's here", and "what does life look like after release". Rork is fast to produce output, so real-world operational reports remain scarce — and that scarcity is exactly where reader attention is concentrating.
