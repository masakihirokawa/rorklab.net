## The evening I had both tabs open

I once sat with Rork and Replit open side by side for about an hour, trying to prototype a new settings screen for one of my wallpaper apps. Both home pages said, more or less, "describe it and get an app," and from the headlines alone I couldn't tell them apart.

Both products have changed a lot since then. As of November 2026, **the two of them write your "mobile app" in completely different languages.** Rork builds iPhone apps in Swift and Android apps in Kotlin. Replit builds mobile apps with Expo (React Native). That single difference shapes how far you can get for free, how you reach the stores, and whether you can switch later.

Here's the short answer up front: **if shipping to the App Store and Google Play is the main goal, start with Rork; if you want web and server code to grow in the same place, start with Replit.** What I'd gently push back on is the idea that you can always "just move it over later." Below is why, and how to check what's actually inside your project before you switch.

I checked prices and screen names on each vendor's official pages on November 26, 2026. Please take one more look at the official pages before you subscribe.

If I had to compress it to one sentence: **choose Rork when you want native SwiftUI and Kotlin code, and Replit when you want one codebase for iPhone, Android and web.** The rest of this piece is for anyone stuck in between.

## Free covers less than it used to

An earlier version of this article said Rork let you try things for free a few times a day. That's no longer the whole story. Rork's official pricing page states that **the Free plan runs in Design mode only, and Build mode — the step that produces working code — needs a paid plan.**

Design mode shows you proposed screens and a look. To get an app you can tap through in the preview, you need Rork Max, from $20 a month. The Max tiers ($20, $200, $500, $900, $1,800) differ only in credits; every tier has the same features.

Replit changed too. Its docs page for the Starter plan says that **as of November 2026, new builders can't pick Starter; eligible people get a 3-day free trial of Core instead.** The in-editor iOS Simulator and Android Emulator are also for Core and above.

So neither one takes you all the way to a published app for free. What worked for me was this: **on Rork, settle the screens in free Design mode before moving to the $20 tier; on Replit, use the 3-day trial to see a working build in one sitting.** I wrote about how Rork credits drain in "[Rork Pricing Compared Honestly: Free vs Rork Max (from $20)](/en/articles/rork-basics/rork-pricing-plan-honest-comparison-2026)."

## The road to the stores, side by side

Publishing is where the two feel most different in the hand. Both assume you've joined the Apple Developer Program (the paid developer membership), and there's no way around that.

Rork handles both the App Store and Google Play from its publish menu. For Android, Rork's official page says it signs the App Bundle (the file Play accepts) and sends the release to Google Play. Its docs also spell out Google's own rule for new personal Play accounts: a closed test with 12 testers for 14 days.

On Replit, "Start publishing to the App Store" in the Publishing tab opens Expo's "Launch" flow inside the editor. You confirm an Expo project name, sign in with Apple, choose the app name and bundle ID, and Replit sets up certificates, builds in the cloud and submits to TestFlight beta review. The official tutorial is clear that **the bundle ID can't be changed after the first build.**

## Three misunderstandings worth untangling

**The first is "Rork is a React Native (Expo) tool."** It used to be. Today Rork doesn't create new projects in Expo; its own comparison page says iPhone is Swift, Android is Kotlin and web is React. Existing Expo projects can still be built and published. I covered what that switch means in "[Rork No Longer Creates New Expo Projects: Keep Yours, or Rebuild It in SwiftUI?](/en/articles/rork-basics/rork-expo-no-longer-new-keep-or-rebuild-swiftui)"

**The second is "Replit can't take a mobile app to the stores."** That's out of date as well. Replit now has a "Mobile app" type, with official steps for TestFlight and the App Store. Because it sits on Expo underneath, you'll meet an Expo project name and slug partway through.

**The third is "once it's on GitHub, I can move it to the other tool as is."** The two write different languages, so Rork's Swift code won't load as a Replit Expo project. Going the other way, Rork reads Replit's Expo code and rebuilds it in Swift and Kotlin. Rork's own migration guide says pixel-perfect, one-to-one parity isn't the goal.

## Check what you exported before you switch

With Rork's November 25, 2026 update, **projects you sync to GitHub or download now run outside Rork.** An exported iPhone project includes Rork's public addresses in its settings file, and an exported Android project compiles as is. Passwords and secret keys are not included.

I tripped over this once. When I tried to bring screens I'd built in Replit into Rork, I assumed the code would just work, and it took me about half a day to realise the blank screen came from how environment variables were loaded. If I'd first asked **"what is this project made of, and what did the export leave out?"**, I'd likely have saved most of that afternoon.

Since then, the first thing I do with an exported folder is run it through the script below. It tells Expo from Swift from Kotlin, lists what the next tool will need, and points at anything that looks like a secret.

```bash
#!/usr/bin/env bash
# Usage: bash stack-check.sh <project folder>
# Shows what an exported project is made of, and what the next tool will need
set -u
DIR="${1:-.}"
[ -d "$DIR" ] || { echo "Folder not found: $DIR"; exit 2; }
cd "$DIR"

has() { find . -path ./node_modules -prune -o "$@" -print -quit 2>/dev/null | grep -q .; }

echo "== What this project is: $DIR =="
STACK=""
if [ -f package.json ] && grep -q '"expo"' package.json; then
  STACK="expo"; echo "Expo (React Native): expo is listed in package.json"
fi
if has -name '*.xcodeproj'; then
  STACK="${STACK} swift"; echo "Swift (Xcode project): $(find . -name '*.xcodeproj' -print -quit)"
fi
if has \( -name 'build.gradle.kts' -o -name 'build.gradle' \); then
  STACK="${STACK} kotlin"; echo "Android (Gradle): $(find . -path ./node_modules -prune -o \( -name 'build.gradle.kts' -o -name 'build.gradle' \) -print -quit)"
fi
[ -z "$STACK" ] && echo "Can't tell (no package.json, .xcodeproj or build.gradle)"

echo "== What the next tool will need =="
case "$STACK" in
  *expo*) echo "Replit: same Expo stack as its Mobile app type, so the code can likely come along"
          echo "Rork  : a GitHub import is rebuilt in Swift / Kotlin (it won't be the same code)" ;;
esac
case "$STACK" in
  *swift*|*kotlin*) echo "Xcode / Android Studio: opens as is (run xed . in the iPhone app folder)"
                    echo "Replit: its Mobile app type is Expo, so expect a rebuild from screens and spec" ;;
esac

echo "== Values the export leaves out (you put these back) =="
HITS=$(grep -rIlE '(SECRET|API_KEY|PRIVATE_KEY|SERVICE_ROLE)' . \
  --exclude-dir=node_modules --exclude-dir=.git 2>/dev/null | head -20)
if [ -n "$HITS" ]; then echo "$HITS" | sed 's/^/check: /'; else echo "No secret-looking names found"; fi
ls -a | grep -E '^\.env' | sed 's/^/env file: /' || true
```

To test it, I made two sample folders on Linux with bash 5: one shaped like a Rork export (an iPhone folder and an Android folder) and one Expo project with a `.env`, standing in for the Replit side. Here's the output exactly as it came out.

The language check comes first because it decides whether you're carrying code over or rebuilding, and those two differ by an order of magnitude in effort. Secrets come last because the November 25 notes say plainly that they stay out of the export, and that's where the next tool stops you first. If a real Rork export uses different folder names from my sample, the result doesn't change: the script only looks for `.xcodeproj` and `build.gradle.kts`. ## Which one — where I draw the line

Here's how I'd turn all of that into a decision.

- **If the app leans on iPhone features** — widgets, Apple Watch, games — choose Rork. You keep real Swift code, and since November 25 the export opens in Xcode with `xed .`
- **If you want web and mobile from one codebase**, or you want to write server logic yourself in detail, choose Replit. It's Expo code, so anyone comfortable with React will find it readable
- **If you still can't decide**, settle the screens in Rork's free Design mode, then give the same screen description to Replit during its 3-day trial, and see which one stops your hands less often. That's the cheapest comparison I know

There's one more angle that matters for client work or apps you'll run for years: who takes it over later. Will you look for someone who reads both Swift and Kotlin, or someone who reads one Expo codebase? Neither is wrong. **Picture the person who'll inherit the code, and pick the stack they can read** — that's the choice I've regretted least.

## The next thing you'll hit: your first Git commit

Whichever you pick, once you start fixing things locally or switching tools, the first wall is knowing what must never go into Git. Once a secret lands in history, it's tedious to get out again. I wrote the steps in "[Write .gitignore Before You Run git init on an Exported Rork Project](/en/articles/rork-basics/rork-export-gitignore-before-git-init)."

**Pick the tool backwards from where you'll publish and who will inherit the code.** I try to hold to that order however shiny the next feature is. A good first step is to run your own project through the script above and find out whether you're carrying code over or rebuilding.
