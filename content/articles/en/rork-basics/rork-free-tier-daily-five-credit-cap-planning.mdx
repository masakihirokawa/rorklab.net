When I pick up a new tool, the first thing I count is not the feature list. It is whether I can burn through the free allowance in a single sitting. If I can, I learn within a day whether the tool suits me. If I cannot, my judgment gets spread across a week whether I like it or not.

I was recounting Rork's free allowance one evening and stopped. Thirty-five credits a month. On its own that sounds like enough to assemble one small app. Then I read the daily cap — five — and the premise flipped. When I put my billing screen next to the official docs, there was a second surprise: on the free plan, the only thing that runs is Design mode, where Rork proposes screens and visual direction.

Thirty-five is not a pool you can dip into freely. It is **five, seven times over**. And what the free tier lets you test is not "can I build this" but "what exactly should I build." Knowing those two things before day one changes how the free week goes.

Older write-ups describe a "Pro" tier at $25 for 100 credits. The current pricing page lists only Free and Rork Max; the old Pro plan survives only on accounts created before August 4, 2026. If you budget from an older summary, this is where the numbers stop matching.

## Five a day is a ceiling, not a count

Rork used to charge one credit per exchange. The official wording now is "A message costs what its work costs": the amount of work a message triggers decides what it costs. When it is paid from the free monthly allowance, every message costs at least one credit.

So five a day does not mean five messages. It means at most five. A message that rebuilds several screens at once, or pulls in web search or image generation, can cost more than one. You can see what each message actually cost from the "⋯" menu under the reply.

There is a second trap if you start mid-month. Near the end of the month, the daily cap can no longer reach the full 35. Start on November 25 and you have six days left including today; five times six is thirty, and the remaining five vanish on December 1.

Doing that arithmetic by hand every time gets old, so I turned it into a script. It runs as-is with Node.js.

```js
#!/usr/bin/env node
// free-credit-pace.mjs — how many credits you can still spend this month on Rork's free tier (35/month, 5/day)
// Usage: node free-credit-pace.mjs <used this month> [<used today>] [YYYY-MM-DD]
// Limits are the official figures as of November 2026 (docs.rork.com). Update them if they change.
const MONTHLY = 35; // monthly allowance in Japan and other major regions
const DAILY = 5;    // daily cap

const [usedMonthArg = "0", usedTodayArg = "0", dateArg] = process.argv.slice(2);
const usedMonth = Number(usedMonthArg);
const usedToday = Number(usedTodayArg);

if ([usedMonth, usedToday].some((n) => Number.isNaN(n) || n < 0) ||
    usedToday > DAILY || usedMonth > MONTHLY || usedToday > usedMonth) {
  console.error("Usage: node free-credit-pace.mjs <used this month> [<used today>] [YYYY-MM-DD]");
  process.exit(1);
}

const today = dateArg ? new Date(`${dateArg}T00:00:00`) : new Date();
if (Number.isNaN(today.getTime())) {
  console.error("Pass the date as YYYY-MM-DD");
  process.exit(1);
}

const daysInMonth = new Date(today.getFullYear(), today.getMonth() + 1, 0).getDate();
const daysLeft = daysInMonth - today.getDate() + 1; // days remaining, today included

const capByDaily = (DAILY - usedToday) + (daysLeft - 1) * DAILY; // what the daily cap still allows
const capByMonthly = MONTHLY - usedMonth;                         // what the monthly allowance still allows
const usable = Math.min(capByDaily, capByMonthly);

console.log(`Days left (including today): ${daysLeft}`);
console.log(`Left under the daily cap: ${capByDaily} / left under the monthly allowance: ${capByMonthly}`);
console.log(`Most you can still spend this month: ${usable} (binding limit: ${capByDaily < capByMonthly ? "daily cap" : "monthly allowance"})`);
console.log(`Expected to expire unused at month end: ${capByMonthly - usable}`);
```

Running it looks like this:

```text
$ node free-credit-pace.mjs 0 0 2026-11-25
Days left (including today): 6
Left under the daily cap: 30 / left under the monthly allowance: 35
Most you can still spend this month: 30 (binding limit: daily cap)
Expected to expire unused at month end: 5
```

The script computes the two limits separately and takes the smaller one because they trade places over the month. If it is the 10th and you have already used 12, the monthly allowance is what binds, and the daily cap stops mattering. One thing I could not confirm in the official docs is which time zone the reset follows. The script counts by your local date, so I would leave yourself some margin on the last day of the month.

## I rebuilt my plan around "free means Design mode"

For a while I treated the free allowance as a small budget — enough, I assumed, to have Rork generate a skeleton, try it on my phone, and fix what I found. That framing did not work well for me.

On day one I asked for a working skeleton and found that building and previewing both sit on the paid side. More than half of my five-day plan was work the free tier could not do. It was the evening of day three before I admitted the problem was not the allowance; my plan had never looked at what the free tier covers.

The line I drew from that: **I spend the free five not on building, but on making sure day one of a paid plan has no open questions.** If the screens and the flow are settled in Design mode, the first paid build is only a matter of turning decisions into something that runs. The more indecision I burn off for free, the fewer paid credits go to rework.

## A five-day allocation

This is the allocation I use. The part that matters most is the stop condition: once a day's question has an answer, I close the tab and leave the rest of that day's credits alone.

</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>1</td><td>How many screens?</td><td>The goal and every screen name you can think of</td><td>The screen count and names are fixed</td></tr>

<tr><td>3</td><td>What goes on each screen?</td><td>Fields for list and detail, and the empty state</td><td>Field names match the names of the data you will store</td></tr>
<tr><td>4</td><td>Visual direction</td><td>Color, text size, whether it is used in the dark</td><td>You have one reference screen you want to follow</td></tr>
<tr><td>5</td><td>Go paid or not?</td><td>A request to review all proposals so far, end to end</td><td>You know whether you still want to work on this app</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

Day three's rule — make the field names match the data names — pays off later. If they drift apart, every Build-mode message that wires a screen to its data has to restate the mapping, and each of those messages does more work and costs more.

## Write the whole spec before you send anything

Most of my credit loss came from round trips, not from feature count. "Build this," "not like that," "try this instead" — every undecided detail turns into another message.

Before, my first message looked like this:

```text
I want to make a budgeting app. Please give it a nice design.
```

That leaves Rork to guess the number of screens and the target device, and every wrong guess costs another message. Now I draft about 200 words in a plain text editor before I send anything. After:

```text
# Goal
(Who, in what situation, can do what in a single action)

# Target device
iPhone only (ignore Android and iPad for now)

# Screens
1. List: what is shown, in what order / what appears when empty
2. Detail: fields displayed / fields editable
3. Settings: only the values that can change

# What I want to decide today
(Exactly one question from the five-day table above)

# Out of scope for now
- Notifications, payments, sharing, sign-in (leave them out of the proposal)
```

The line that earned its keep was the last one. Without it, screens I never asked for arrive anyway, and removing them costs another message. "What I want to decide today" is limited to one question for the same reason: a message that does less work is more likely to stay at the one-credit minimum. For concrete examples of the first lines that save trouble later, see [three one-line additions to your first budget-app prompt](/articles/rork-basics/kakeibo-first-prompt-yen-integer-local-month-input-normalize-three-checks).

## Three common misreadings

**"Free is a trial that eventually stops."** The current free plan needs no card and does not expire. The allowance comes back on the 1st of each month.

**"I can spend all 35 on whichever day I like."** The daily cap means five at most in a day. If you start late in the month, you cannot use all 35.

**"I can build on free and decide after trying it on my phone."** As of November 2026, free runs Design mode only; building, on-device testing and publishing assume Rork Max. The free five days go further when they are spent deciding what to build.

My own rule: **if on day five I still want to be working on the same app, I move to the $20 option. If not, I change the idea.** It is less a judgment about price than a test of whether I have the energy to continue. If interest faded by day three, I take that as a signal about the idea rather than the tool.

## What you run into on your first paid day

Once the design is settled and you move to a paid plan, two things tend to come up first.

The first is how heavy a Build-mode message is. It does more work than a Design-mode message, so the model you choose changes how fast credits go. I logged which kind of fix suits which model in [choosing the model by the kind of fix, with a week of credit logging](/articles/rork-ai/rork-model-menu-three-fix-types-credit-destination-ledger).

The second is the names you cannot change later. Your Bundle ID or package name is locked once the first build is uploaded to the store. Checking [the names to settle before your first release](/articles/rork-basics/rork-app-id-immutable-before-first-release) alongside the app name you chose in Design mode makes that first paid day much calmer.

## Decide tomorrow's five tonight

If there is one thing to do today, pick tomorrow's question from the five-day table and write it down together with its stop condition. Keep it small enough to stop on, and your morning self will not spend the first message wandering.

I could not make the free tier work while I thought of it as a trial period. It started working once I treated it as a place to make the decisions my first paid day would need. I hope your five go further than mine did at the start.
