## #1: Layering Rewarded, Interstitial, and App Open to Lift ARPDAU by 1.4×

[How I Layered Rewarded, Interstitial, and App Open Ads to Lift ARPDAU 1.4× on My Wallpaper App](/articles/rork-business/rork-rewarded-interstitial-appopen-three-layer-arpdau-design)

The most-read article this week stopped treating ad formats as independent optimizations and redesigned them as one three-layer system. Rewarded carries "feature unlock," Interstitial carries "session transition," App Open carries "return-from-background monetization" — and the frequency rules for each reference the others. ARPDAU on the wallpaper app moved up by 1.4×.

When I only optimized Rewarded in isolation, Interstitial frequency was hard to read and users started to drift. With the three-layer setup, conditions like "if the user saw Rewarded in the last X minutes, skip Interstitial" become trivial to write. The article shares the actual frequency-rule JSON and a sample of how the Rork-generated code reads it.

```json
// Simplified frequency-rule excerpt
{
  "interstitial": {
    "min_session_seconds": 60,
    "skip_if_rewarded_within": 300
  },
  "app_open": {
    "min_background_seconds": 30,
    "skip_if_interstitial_within": 120
  }
}
```

## #2: Adding StoreKit 2 In-App Purchases to a Rork iOS App

[Implementation Notes: Adding StoreKit 2 In-App Purchases to a Rork iOS App](/articles/rork-dev/rork-storekit-2-in-app-purchase-implementation-memo)

For one-time IAP on Rork-generated iOS apps, StoreKit 2 ends up being the clearest path. The article walks the whole flow on async/await — product fetch, purchase, transaction observation, restore — and shows how to layer this on top of what Rork emits, with samples.

In my case, I had to roll the same IAP logic across four wallpaper apps, so I extracted the shared layer as a Swift Package and let each Rork project consume it through the Package. The post covers that boundary decision, along with the spots that tripped me up in Sandbox and TestFlight.

## #3: iOS Memory Pressure on Rork Apps — A 5-Tier Release Architecture

[iOS Memory Pressure on Rork Apps — A 5-Tier Release Architecture from 12 Years of Wallpaper Apps](/articles/rork-dev/rork-ios-memory-pressure-5tier-release-architecture)

Wallpaper apps handle a lot of images, so memory hygiene directly drives crash rate. The article organizes what I've sharpened over 12 years of indie development into five tiers: as memory pressure escalates from warning to critical to emergency, which buffers get released first, and which last.

The order matters. Release the wrong tier first — UIImage caches, lazy-load intermediates, WebView DOM, background-task scratch — and you end up evicting the image the user is currently looking at. Drop this 5-tier scheme into the Rork-generated code, top to bottom, and you stop losing users on older devices.

## #4: Hitting Monthly Break-Even on a 100-Yen-per-Month App Built with Rork

[From a Rork-Built App to Monthly Break-Even at 100 Yen Subscription: Indie Numbers from May 2026](/articles/rork-business/rork-100yen-monthly-app-breakeven-admob-revenuecat-may-2026-actual-metrics)

"Can a 100-yen monthly subscription actually break even?" — this article answers with May's real numbers. AdMob alongside, RevenueCat trial configuration, churn-timing distribution, and support hours all included, laid out as close to raw as I could get.

The short answer: even at 100 yen monthly, with AdMob alongside and the right trial setup, an indie shop running one app can hit break-even. The single biggest watch-out is that support workload comes in heavier than the spreadsheet predicts. The post documents how I trimmed that "unexpected cost" line back, with concrete examples.

## #5: Adding AppLovin MAX to a Rork-Generated iOS Project

[Adding AppLovin MAX to a Rork-Generated iOS Project — A Field Note on Init Order, ATT Consent, and eCPM A/B Testing](/articles/app-dev/rork-applovin-max-ios-integration-att-ecpm-notes)

A retrofit note on adding AppLovin MAX to a Rork-generated project. Get the initialization order wrong and the mediation SDK starts firing before the ATT consent dialog appears — fill drops badly as a result. The article documents the trap and the reliable fix.

After AppLovin MAX is in production, eCPM validation works better as a layered exercise, in the same spirit as #1. Rork builds the foundation fast; monetization logic gets layered on after — that division of labor fits the pace of indie development well.
