One morning I put my own billing screen next to Rork's official pricing page and they didn't line up. I remembered a ladder of three or so paid tiers. The page showed two things: "Free" and "Rork Max". And Max now started at $20 a month, an order of magnitude below the number in my notes.

If I'd kept calculating from those notes, I might have talked myself into paying for something I didn't need. Pricing goes stale on the day you learn it, and that morning was a small, cold reminder of it.

Here's the short version: **as of November 2026, Rork works in two steps: design for free, then build and ship on Rork Max.** Which Max option you pick, and how you cover the months where you run short, can change your monthly bill several times over for the same amount of work. Everything below uses Rork's own documentation, and I'll walk through the decision one piece at a time.

The one thing I'd want you to take from this table: **every Rork Max option has exactly the same features; only the credit amount differs.** The docs say so directly: "Every Rork Max option includes the full feature set." Paying $200 doesn't unlock anything $20 doesn't.

Work out the price per credit and something else shows up. The $20 through $500 options all come to $0.20 per credit; the $900 and $1,800 options to $0.18. A top-up credit costs $0.22. In other words, **moving up a tier barely lowers your unit price.** That fact is what the "$20 plus top-ups" approach later in this piece rests on.

India also has an entry tier, Rork Start (₹649/month, 40 credits, light models only). It isn't available elsewhere.

## What the Free plan can and can't do

You can stay on Free indefinitely with no card on file; it isn't a trial that expires. But as of November 2026, Free runs **Design mode only**.

In Design mode you describe the app and Rork proposes the screens and the look, for iPhone, Android, web, iPad, Apple Watch, or Vision Pro. Turning that design into a working app (Build mode), testing it on a device, and publishing to the App Store or Google Play all require a paid plan.

Free credits have two limits: 35 a month in major markets, and no more than 5 a day. They reset at the start of each calendar month. Because of the daily cap, you can't save them up and spend all 35 in one mid-month burst. I wrote about living with that daily cap separately in [Rork's free tier gives you 35 credits a month, but the 5-a-day cap is what you'll hit](/en/articles/rork-basics/rork-free-tier-daily-five-credit-cap-planning).

What I try to do on Free is **settle every design question there.** Which screens exist, how they connect, what gets saved. Anything left undecided turns into rework once you're building, and rework is paid for in credits.

## A credit is no longer "one per message"

### Which credits go first, and which ones survive

It's one balance, but three kinds of credit sit inside it, and they're spent in a fixed order.

<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>Kind</th><th>Where it comes from</th><th>Resets monthly?</th><th>Spend order</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Subscription credits</td><td>Free monthly credits and your plan's credits</td><td>Yes (no carry-over)</td><td>1 (free) then 2 (plan)</td></tr>
<tr><td>Reward credits</td><td>Referrals, connecting RevenueCat, and similar</td><td>No</td><td>3</td></tr>
<tr><td>Usage credits</td><td>Top-ups ($0.22 each) and old Cloud Credits</td><td>No</td><td>4</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

The credits that expire are spent first; the ones you bought last the longest. Until October 30, 2026 there was a separate "Cloud Credits" balance. It was folded into Usage credits at the same value ($1 = 5 credits).

The reset date differs too. **Paid plans renew on the monthly anniversary of your first purchase**; Free resets on the 1st. If you assume everything refills on the 1st, your end-of-month calls will be off by up to a few weeks.

## Start at $20 and cover short months with top-ups

Put three facts together: unit prices are nearly flat, plan credits expire, and bought credits don't. The order of decisions follows from that. **While your monthly usage is still unpredictable, staying on the $20 option and topping up in the months you run short usually costs less than moving up a tier.**

The crossover is one division. The $180 gap to the $200 option, divided by $0.22 per top-up credit, is about 818 credits. Add the 100 included at $20 and you get **roughly 918 credits a month.** Below that, $20 plus top-ups wins; if you're consistently above it, the $200 option does.

I turned it into a script so I can drop in each month's number. It runs as-is with Node.js.

```js
#!/usr/bin/env node
// plan-vs-topup.mjs — given monthly credit usage, find the cheapest Rork Max option plus top-ups.
// Prices are from docs.rork.com/introduction/subscriptions as of November 2026. Update them if Rork changes pricing.
// Usage: node plan-vs-topup.mjs <credits used per month> [<credits used per month> ...]
const OPTIONS = [
  { usd: 20, credits: 100 },
  { usd: 200, credits: 1000 },
  { usd: 500, credits: 2500 },
  { usd: 900, credits: 5000 },
  { usd: 1800, credits: 10000 },
];
const TOPUP_USD = 0.22; // one top-up credit (carries over to next month)

const needs = process.argv.slice(2).map(Number);
if (needs.length === 0 || needs.some(Number.isNaN)) {
  console.error("Usage: node plan-vs-topup.mjs <credits used per month> [...]");
  process.exit(1);
}
for (const need of needs) {
  const rows = OPTIONS.map((o) => {
    const short = Math.max(need - o.credits, 0);
    const cost = o.usd + short * TOPUP_USD;
    return { ...o, short, cost, unused: Math.max(o.credits - need, 0) };
  }).sort((a, b) => a.cost - b.cost);
  const best = rows[0];
  const line = best.short > 0
    ? `$${best.usd} option + ${best.short} top-up credits`
    : `$${best.usd} option only (${best.unused} credits expire at renewal)`;
  console.log(`${String(need).padStart(5)} credits/mo → cheapest $${best.cost.toFixed(2)}: ${line}`);
}
```

Here it is with a range of monthly usage:

```
$ node plan-vs-topup.mjs 80 150 300 600 900 920 1200 2000
   80 credits/mo → cheapest $20.00: $20 option only (20 credits expire at renewal)
  150 credits/mo → cheapest $31.00: $20 option + 50 top-up credits
  300 credits/mo → cheapest $64.00: $20 option + 200 top-up credits
  600 credits/mo → cheapest $130.00: $20 option + 500 top-up credits
  900 credits/mo → cheapest $196.00: $20 option + 800 top-up credits
  920 credits/mo → cheapest $200.00: $200 option only (80 credits expire at renewal)
 1200 credits/mo → cheapest $244.00: $200 option + 200 top-up credits
 2000 credits/mo → cheapest $420.00: $200 option + 1000 top-up credits
```

At 300 credits a month, $20 plus 200 top-up credits comes to $64. Moving up to the $200 option would have cost $136 more, and 700 credits would have vanished at the renewal date.

One caveat. Top-ups are sold in fixed amounts, from +25 credits ($5.50) to +1,000 credits ($220.00), not one credit at a time as the script assumes. You'll round up a little, but since bought credits carry over, the extra isn't wasted. Auto top-up, if you turn it on, adds $20 each time you run out; it has a monthly cap you can set, and I'd turn the cap on together with it.

### A mistake I made, and the line I drew after it

I'll be honest: I once moved up a tier for a heavy build month and then forgot to come back down. The next month I only touched a few strings of copy, and most of the credits were still sitting there when the renewal date came. It wasn't the amount that bothered me so much as realizing I'd been paying without looking.

My rule now is simple: **move up a tier on what I've used, never on what I expect to use.** I only move to the $200 option after two straight months above the ~918 line. Until then, $20 and top-ups absorb the spikes.

## Pace your credits back from the renewal date

Even with top-ups as a safety net, you can't feel mid-month whether you'll make it. About twelve days after my renewal date I run the next script once with three numbers. The trick is to count elapsed days **from your own renewal date**, not from the 1st.

```js
#!/usr/bin/env node
// credit-pace.mjs — turn non-rolling plan credits into a daily allowance until the next renewal.
// Paid plans renew on the anniversary of your first purchase; Free resets on the 1st of each month.
// Usage: node credit-pace.mjs <credits per month> <days since renewal> <used> [cycle days] [--free]
const args = process.argv.slice(2);
const isFree = args.includes("--free");
const [total, elapsed, used, cycleDays = 30] = args.filter((a) => a !== "--free").map(Number);
if ([total, elapsed, used].some(Number.isNaN)) {
  console.error("Usage: node credit-pace.mjs <credits per month> <days since renewal> <used> [cycle days] [--free]");
  process.exit(1);
}
const remain = total - used;
const daysLeft = Math.max(cycleDays - elapsed, 1);
const pace = used / Math.max(elapsed, 1);     // current spend per day
const landing = Math.round(pace * cycleDays); // where this pace ends up
const allowance = remain / daysLeft;          // per-day amount that uses everything

console.log(`Remaining : ${remain} credits / ${daysLeft} days left`);
console.log(`Pace      : ${pace.toFixed(2)} / day → lands at ${landing} (cap ${total})`);
console.log(`Allowance : ${allowance.toFixed(2)} / day (${(allowance * 7).toFixed(1)} / week)`);
if (isFree && allowance > 5) {
  console.log(`Note: Free is capped at 5 credits a day. Anything above 5/day will expire unused.`);
}
if (landing > total) {
  console.log(`⚠ You'll run out around day ${Math.floor(total / pace)}. Check for a simulator left running and repeated rewordings first.`);
} else if (landing < total * 0.7) {
  console.log(`△ About ${total - landing} credits will expire unused (plan credits don't carry over).`);
} else {
  console.log(`✔ On pace to use your credits.`);
}
```

On the $20 option (100 credits), twelve days in with 70 already spent:

```
$ node credit-pace.mjs 100 12 70
Remaining : 30 credits / 18 days left
Pace      : 5.83 / day → lands at 175 (cap 100)
Allowance : 1.67 / day (11.7 / week)
⚠ You'll run out around day 17. Check for a simulator left running and repeated rewordings first.
```

Running out around day 17 is a concrete date, and having one helps you decide calmly between topping up and pushing work to next month. And in my experience, months like this are usually driven by a simulator left on rather than by the prompts. At 12 credits an hour, it keeps draining while you sit and think.

Now the other side: the Free plan, twelve days in, only 6 credits used.

```
$ node credit-pace.mjs 35 12 6 30 --free
Remaining : 29 credits / 18 days left
Pace      : 0.50 / day → lands at 15 (cap 35)
Allowance : 1.61 / day (11.3 / week)
△ About 20 credits will expire unused (plan credits don't carry over).
```

Twenty credits are on track to expire unused. Free credits are capped at 5 a day, so you can't catch up at the end of the month. If there are screens you want to refine, spread the work across earlier days.

### Put more into each request

Even with cost tied to work rather than message count, fewer round trips still help. Splitting one screen into three requests means three rounds of re-reading context, and often three builds.

```
❌ Three round trips
1. "Make a settings screen"
2. "Add a dark mode toggle"
3. "Save the toggle state"

✅ One request
"Make a settings screen with a dark mode toggle. Save the selected
state on the device and restore it on the next launch. Put a title
at the top and list the settings vertically below it."
```

I don't have enough measurements to claim the combined request is always cheaper than three. What I can say is that on days I batch requests, the per-message cost in the "⋯" menu is easier to read, and I miss my month-end landing less often.

## Common misunderstandings, corrected

**"Rork Max is the $200 premium product."** As of November 2026, Rork Max is the only paid plan for new accounts, it starts at $20, and the options differ only in credits ("Every Rork Max option includes the full feature set, from $20/mo"). Comparing on the old "Max = $200" assumption pushes people toward a $200 option they don't need.

**"You can ship to the stores on Free."** Free is Design mode only: no building, no publishing. The official FAQ says building, testing, and publishing a working app needs Rork Max.

**"Rork builds Expo (React Native) apps."** New projects are Swift for iPhone, Kotlin for Android, and React for web. You can no longer create Expo projects, but existing ones keep building, publishing, and exporting to GitHub ([What happened to Expo?](https://docs.rork.com/expo)). If you have one, [Rork no longer creates Expo projects: keep yours, or rebuild in SwiftUI?](/en/articles/rork-basics/rork-expo-no-longer-new-keep-or-rebuild-swiftui) lays out how I'd decide.

On annual billing: the official pricing documentation as of November 2026 lists monthly options only, and I couldn't find an annual option in what I checked. I'd hold off on any math that assumes an annual discount until you can confirm one on the official page.

## How far ad revenue gets you, tier by tier

Whether a tool is expensive is easier to judge once you turn it into the number of users needed to pay for it. I've run ad-supported wallpaper apps for a long time, so I tend to translate any monthly fee into "how many DAU is that?"

Conservative assumptions: 3 ad impressions per daily active user per day, 30 days, and eCPMs of $1, $2, and $5.

```js
#!/usr/bin/env node
// breakeven.mjs — translate each Rork Max option's monthly price into the DAU needed to cover it.
// Replace eCPM and impressions per DAU with your own AdMob numbers.
const OPTIONS = [20, 200, 500];   // Rork Max options as of November 2026 (USD/month)
const IMPRESSIONS_PER_DAU = 3;    // ad impressions per DAU per day (a modest value)
const DAYS = 30;
const ECPMS = [1, 2, 5];          // low / typical / good

for (const ecpm of ECPMS) {
  const perDau = (IMPRESSIONS_PER_DAU * DAYS * ecpm) / 1000;
  const cells = OPTIONS.map((usd) => `$${usd}: ${Math.ceil(usd / perDau).toLocaleString("en-US")} DAU`);
  console.log(`eCPM $${ecpm} ($${perDau.toFixed(3)} per DAU per month) → ${cells.join(" / ")}`);
}
```

```
$ node breakeven.mjs
eCPM $1 ($0.090 per DAU per month) → $20: 223 DAU / $200: 2,223 DAU / $500: 5,556 DAU
eCPM $2 ($0.180 per DAU per month) → $20: 112 DAU / $200: 1,112 DAU / $500: 2,778 DAU
eCPM $5 ($0.450 per DAU per month) → $20: 45 DAU / $200: 445 DAU / $500: 1,112 DAU
```

The $20 option needs about 112 DAU at a $2 eCPM, which one steadily used app can reach. The $200 option only makes sense once four-digit DAU is in sight.

The part that's easy to miss is Rork AI Cloud inside your app. Each time a user calls AI, the provider cost ($1 = 5 credits) comes out of your balance. **Subtract per-DAU AI cost before you look at break-even.** If your app has AI features, check the Cloud Usage numbers at least once a month.

The `IMPRESSIONS_PER_DAU` value of 3 is deliberately modest. Raising it improves the table, but it also raises churn. Lowering break-even by bumping that constant tends to be an improvement that only exists on paper.

## Pay for what you need, when you need it

Here's how the numbers above turn into my own sequence.

1. **Settle the design on Free.** Decide screens, navigation, and what gets saved in Design mode, a little each day within the 5-credit cap.
2. **Move to the $20 option the day you start building.** Not the night before a deadline. If something snags in checkout, you want room to sort it out.
3. **Absorb short months with top-ups.** Bought credits carry over, so buying a little extra just moves it to next month.
4. **Consider the $200 option only after two straight months above ~918.** Not before.

Shipping also involves costs outside Rork. Enrolling in the Apple Developer Program is something Rork can't do for you; I covered those steps in [The Apple Developer account steps Rork can't automate](/en/articles/rork-basics/rork-apple-developer-account-steps-rork-cannot-automate). For the order in which bills arrive in your first year, see [The first-year bill order: Apple, Google, EAS, Supabase](/en/articles/rork-business/rork-first-year-bill-order-apple-google-eas-supabase-timeline). Once pricing is settled, those procedures are where people tend to get stuck.

## The next snag: once you ship AI features, you have two things drawing on one balance

Right after you publish on the $20 option, the thing that tends to catch people is in-app AI billing. The credits you spend building and the credits your users spend calling AI come out of the same balance. It's invisible before launch and grows with every new user.

During your launch week, set the monthly limit for your apps' AI and the low-balance email (the default threshold is $5). When the limit is reached, only your app's AI stops; building isn't affected.

A pricing table starts aging the day you read it. So today, open Settings → Credits and find the renewal date on the Subscription credits row. Run `credit-pace.mjs` once from that date, and next month's tier decision gets much easier.
