It was the first morning of the month when I opened the Payments page in AdMob. The balance showed a few thousand yen, the bank account showed nothing, and the app had been carrying ads for about three months. I closed the tab, and one question kept following me around for the rest of the day: if this never reaches the threshold, does the balance just quietly expire?

If you are looking at the same screen with the same knot in your stomach, you are far from alone. Small indie apps take a long time to reach a payout, and the dashboard does not explain much on its own. So in this note I'll stick to what Google's AdMob Help pages actually say, and work out two things: what happens to a balance below the threshold, and what really stops a payment.

The short version first. **Falling short of the threshold never wipes your balance by itself.** The things that deserve your attention are deadlines on paperwork and an account where ads have stopped serving altogether.

## The short answer: a balance under the threshold rolls over

The AdMob Help article "Payments and transactions" says that if your monthly earnings don't reach the payment threshold, they are rolled over and combined with the next month's earnings. Once the combined balance passes the threshold, you are paid in one lump sum.

For an account that reports in Japanese yen, the payment threshold is **8,000 yen**; for a US dollar account it is 100 USD. The value depends on the account's reporting currency, and the per-currency table lives in the Help article "Payment thresholds".

One detail is easy to miss. The threshold is not checked against the estimated earnings on your home screen. The Help states plainly that all thresholds are based on your payment history page rather than estimated earnings. Most "I passed 8,000 yen and still wasn't paid" moments come down to exactly this gap.

## The monthly calendar, step by step

AdMob pays on a monthly cycle. For bank transfer (EFT), the Help describes the milestones like this:

<thead>
<tr><th>When</th><th>What happens</th><th>What you do</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Around the 3rd</td><td>Last month's estimated earnings are finalized and posted to the transactions on the Payments page, along with any adjustments for invalid activity</td><td>Check the finalized amount</td></tr>
<tr><td>The 20th</td><td>Only changes made by this date, including removing a payment hold, count for this month's payment. Your balance on the 20th must reach the threshold</td><td>Check for holds and confirm the balance</td></tr>

The exact arrival depends on your time zone, whether the 21st lands on a weekend or holiday, and your form of payment. If you clear a hold after the 20th, the change rolls into the following month's cycle.

Before I understood this calendar, I checked the balance almost every week. Each time the number hadn't moved, I'd start clicking through settings, convinced I had missed something. What I settled on instead is simple: **I open the Payments page twice a month, on the 3rd and the 20th.** One day to read the finalized amount, one day to look for holds. On any other day, there is almost nothing to decide.

## There isn't just one threshold

"The threshold" is really four different numbers with different jobs. Here are the values for a yen account, taken from the Help table as of November 2026.

</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Verification (identity / address)</td><td>$0 / $10 equivalent</td><td>You are asked to verify your identity and your address (PIN)</td></tr>
<tr><td>Payment method selection</td><td>1,000 yen</td><td>You can add a form of payment, such as a bank account</td></tr>

The Help explains the payment method threshold this way: since Google never issues payments below the threshold, it doesn't let you choose a form of payment until your earnings reach that amount. If you tried to add a bank account and the option simply wasn't there, this is almost always why.

## Three common misreadings, corrected

**"Once estimated earnings pass 8,000 yen, I'll be paid that month."**
The check uses the finalized balance on the Payments page. If your estimate crosses 8,000 yen during November, those earnings are finalized around December 3rd, and the payment goes out around December 21st.

**"If my balance stays small, it eventually expires."**
A balance under the threshold rolls over. What decides whether you lose access is not the size of the balance but two other things covered below: six months without ad serving, and the conditions for cancelling.

**"If my account gets deactivated, the remaining earnings are gone."**
According to "About deactivated AdMob accounts", you can still reach outstanding earnings by reactivating the account, or through the associated AdSense account, as long as you've completed the requirements for a final payment.

## When you're over the threshold and still unpaid

If your balance is above the threshold and nothing has arrived, work down this table. The causes follow the categories in the Help article "Why your AdMob account has a payment hold".

Clicking any hold alert shows the steps to remove it. The Help also says that if a hold kept your balance from being paid in the normal cycle, the payment goes out within a few days of the hold being removed.

## The PIN has a four-month clock

Of everything in that table, the PIN is the one I see overlooked most. The Help article "Address verification (PIN) overview" describes it in this order:

1. When your earnings reach the verification threshold, Google mails a 6-digit PIN to your payments address by standard post, with no tracking number
2. If you've been asked to verify your identity, the PIN isn't sent until that's done
3. It usually takes about three weeks to arrive; if it doesn't, you can request a replacement
4. You have **four months from the date the PIN is generated** to finish address verification

The part people miss is what happens after the deadline. The Help says that if your address isn't verified within four months, **ads stop showing**. The balance doesn't vanish — the thing that earns the balance stops. For a small app, a letter from Google can look like it can wait. I'd rather enter it the day it arrives.

## The real risk is an account that stopped serving

Roll-over is guaranteed, but an inactive account changes the picture. "About deactivated AdMob accounts" says that **AdMob accounts that haven't served any ads for six months are deactivated.** That means zero serving, not low revenue.

To reactivate, the Help lists these steps:

1. Sign in to AdMob and tick the box confirming you'd like the account to be re-approved
2. Click "Reactivate account"
3. Verify your phone number

Google then runs a few checks, which typically take no more than 24 hours. If you verified your address by PIN before, you won't have to do it again. To restart ads in your app, though, you'll need to submit it for an app readiness review, and until it's approved the app shows "Requires review".

## Before you cancel the account

If you're thinking of closing the whole account because an app has been retired, there are further conditions. Per "Cancel your AdMob account", if you don't use AdSense, a final payment requires all of the following:

- A balance above the cancellation threshold (1,000 yen for a yen account)
- A form of payment selected, identity verified, address and payee name verified, and the other steps to getting paid completed
- Any payment holds resolved

If you go ahead without meeting these, you're asked to tick a box confirming you understand you won't be paid the remaining balance. If you do meet them, the final payment arrives within roughly 90 days of the end of the month. An account deactivated for inactivity has to be reactivated before it can be cancelled, and if you keep using the associated AdSense account, the final AdMob payment moves to your AdSense balance instead.

## Three paths, and how to choose

When an app is a long way from the threshold, you have roughly three options.

<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>Option</th><th>What happens to the balance</th><th>Watch out for</th><th>Fits when</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Keep serving and wait</td><td>Rolls over monthly; paid in the month it crosses the threshold</td><td>Finish the PIN within its four months</td><td>Ads are still serving, even a little</td></tr>
<tr><td>Stop updating and leave it</td><td>Account deactivates after six months of zero serving</td><td>Reactivation and a fresh app review remain</td><td>You plan to rebuild the app soon</td></tr>
<tr><td>Cancel the account</td><td>A balance above 1,000 yen is paid out if requirements are met</td><td>Cancelling before meeting them forfeits the balance</td><td>You won't use AdMob again at all</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

If I had to put it in one line: **wait while any app on the account is still serving, and only cancel once you're leaving AdMob for good and the payment requirements are in place.** If several apps share one account, retiring one of them doesn't trigger the six-month rule as long as the others keep serving.

## A small payout estimator

This estimates how many months until you reach 8,000 yen, starting from the finalized balance, and puts the PIN deadline next to it.

```python
import math
from datetime import date

THRESHOLD_JPY = 8000      # payment threshold for yen accounts (AdMob Help, November 2026)
PIN_DEADLINE_MONTHS = 4   # months from PIN generation to complete address verification

def add_months(d: date, months: int) -> date:
    y, m = divmod(d.month - 1 + months, 12)
    return date(d.year + y, m + 1, 1)

def payout_plan(confirmed_balance: int, monthly_confirmed: int, today: date,
                pin_issued: date | None = None) -> dict:
    """Estimate the payout month from the finalized balance and monthly finalized earnings."""
    if monthly_confirmed <= 0:
        raise ValueError("With zero or negative monthly earnings the threshold is never reached")
    remaining = max(THRESHOLD_JPY - confirmed_balance, 0)
    months = math.ceil(remaining / monthly_confirmed)
    # finalized around the 3rd of the next month, paid around the 21st
    payout_month = add_months(today, months + 1)
    plan = {"months_to_threshold": months,
            "expected_payout": f"around {payout_month:%Y-%m}-21"}
    if pin_issued:
        plan["pin_deadline"] = f"by about {add_months(pin_issued, PIN_DEADLINE_MONTHS):%Y-%m}"
    return plan

print(payout_plan(2300, 600, date(2026, 11, 25), pin_issued=date(2026, 10, 10)))
```

Running it on Python 3.10 or later prints:

```text
{'months_to_threshold': 10, 'expected_payout': 'around 2027-10-21', 'pin_deadline': 'by about 2027-02'}
```

In this example, reaching the threshold takes ten months, while the PIN deadline arrives in under half that time. Laid out side by side, it becomes obvious that **the PIN deadline comes long before the money does.** Always feed it the amount from the Payments page, not the estimate. The script rounds to whole months, so treat the PIN date as a rough guide and confirm the exact date in your account.
