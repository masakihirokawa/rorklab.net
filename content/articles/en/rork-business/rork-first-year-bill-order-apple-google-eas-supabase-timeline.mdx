One night I opened a spreadsheet to write down what it would cost to ship an app. Rork, free. Supabase, free. Expo's build service — the thing most of us still call EAS — free. The column added up to roughly zero.

What the sheet didn't have was a column for *when*.

Small amounts still land on the same day, and some services never send a bill at all — they just stop, which is its own way of asking for money. I first wrote this up in October, and when I re-checked every official pricing page on November 20, 2026, one plan name had already changed under me. So this is the revised version: what gets billed in year one, in the order it actually shows up, plus a small script that turns those dates into calendar events.

It's written for you if you've started sketching screens in Rork and want to see, in one pass, where the money starts and which dates you can't afford to forget.

## Bills arrive in roughly the order you work

What surprised me was how neatly the money follows the work itself.

1. **Sketching screens.** Rork Free is enough. It's Design mode only, though, and the 5-credit daily cap tends to bite before the monthly allowance does. I worked through how that cap plays out in [planning around the free tier's daily five-credit cap](/en/articles/rork-basics/rork-free-tier-daily-five-credit-cap-planning).
2. **Wanting a working app.** Your first bill is almost certainly the $20 paid plan, because Rork's docs put Build mode — turning designs into a real app you can test and publish — behind a paid plan. Builds add to the cost of the message that runs them, and the cloud iOS simulator spends 0.2 credits a minute while it's on.
3. **Going to the stores.** This is where Apple's 99 USD and Google's US$25 stack up. The Apple-side steps you have to do yourself are in [what you need to finish on Apple's side before publishing a Rork app](/en/articles/rork-basics/rork-apple-developer-account-steps-rork-cannot-automate).
4. **The last push before release.** Expo Free gives you 15 builds per platform per month. That sounds like plenty, but the pricing page itself says the low-priority queue can wait 90+ minutes at peak times. Waiting time runs out before build count does.
5. **A few weeks after launch.** Time to check that a free Supabase project hasn't paused during a quiet week.

So year-one spending doesn't spread evenly from January to December. It bunches up right before you ship. If you want the waiting days counted too, [breaking down the days you'll spend waiting before release](/en/articles/rork-basics/rork-one-week-release-promise-waiting-days-breakdown) pairs well with this.

Apple's 99 USD comes back every year; Google's US$25 is paid once. Only Apple follows you into year two, and that's worth knowing early.

### When to stay free, and when to move up

For each service with two realistic options, here's the one-line rule I actually use.

  <thead>

    <tr><td>Rork</td><td>You're still comparing screen ideas</td><td>You want it on a device or in a store (Build mode)</td></tr>

**I move Supabase to Pro on the day someone other than me starts opening the app every day.** Deciding by that, rather than by price, has saved me a lot of going back and forth.

## "Billed" and "stopped" are different problems

This is the part I almost missed. I'd been watching invoices, but free tiers end in completely different ways depending on the service.

<table>
  <thead>
    <tr><th>Service</th><th>When the free tier runs out or sits idle</th><th>Does money move?</th></tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Rork</td><td>Credits run out. Wait for the reset or move to a paid plan</td><td>Not unless you upgrade</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Expo</td><td>Once the free builds are used, that month needs Starter</td><td>Not unless you upgrade</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Supabase</td><td>A free project pauses after a week of inactivity</td><td>No bill — but your app's backend stops</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Apple</td><td>If you don't renew, the membership expires</td><td>If you don't pay, distribution stops</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

A Supabase pause comes with no invoice, which is exactly why it's easy to notice late. The app opens, the screens render, and the data just never arrives. Apple is similar: the developer forums are full of people whose apps disappeared from the App Store after a membership lapsed.

**Put the dates when things stop on your calendar before you worry about the bills.** That was the line I drew after this inventory.

I've nearly let a once-a-year renewal slide myself, running my wallpaper apps — "I'll do it next month." It didn't go well. The reminder emails had been filtered into another folder, and I only noticed with a few days left. Since then I keep annual payments as dates, not as amounts.

## Three common misunderstandings

**"I can ship to the stores on Rork Free."** Rork's docs describe Free as Design mode, and say a paid plan is what turns a design into an app you build and can publish. Free to make isn't the same as free to ship.

**"Unused credits roll over."** Your plan's monthly credits don't carry over. Only credits you buy separately ($0.22 each) stay. There's no need to burn them off at month's end, but there's no saving them up either.

**"Supabase Pro is $25 no matter how many projects I have."** The $25 includes compute for one Micro project. Each extra project adds its own compute cost; Supabase's own example puts two Micro projects at $35 a month.

## Turning the dates into a calendar file

When I type these dates in by hand, I reliably drop one. So I wrote a short Python script, standard library only, that writes an `.ics` file you can import into Apple Calendar or Google Calendar.

The problem it solves: yearly, monthly and weekly dates otherwise end up scattered across different inboxes and dashboards.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
# first_year_bills.py — write first-year billing and "stop" dates to an .ics file
# Usage: python3 first_year_bills.py 2026-11-20 2026-12-05 > bills.ics
#   1st arg: the day you started a paid Rork plan / 2nd arg: the day you enrolled with Apple
import sys
import uuid
from datetime import date, datetime, timedelta, timezone

def event(summary, start, rrule=None, alarm_days=0):
    stamp = datetime.now(timezone.utc).strftime("%Y%m%dT%H%M%SZ")
    lines = [
        "BEGIN:VEVENT",
        f"UID:{uuid.uuid4()}@first-year-bills",
        f"DTSTAMP:{stamp}",
        f"DTSTART;VALUE=DATE:{start:%Y%m%d}",
        f"DTEND;VALUE=DATE:{start + timedelta(days=1):%Y%m%d}",
        f"SUMMARY:{summary}",
    ]
    if rrule:
        lines.append(f"RRULE:{rrule}")
    if alarm_days:
        lines += [
            "BEGIN:VALARM",
            "ACTION:DISPLAY",
            f"DESCRIPTION:{summary}",
            f"TRIGGER:-P{alarm_days}D",
            "END:VALARM",
        ]
    lines.append("END:VEVENT")
    return lines

def main():
    if len(sys.argv) != 3:
        sys.exit("usage: first_year_bills.py RORK_PAID_START APPLE_ENROLL")
    rork_paid = date.fromisoformat(sys.argv[1])
    apple = date.fromisoformat(sys.argv[2])
    today = date.today()
    first_of_next = (today.replace(day=1) + timedelta(days=32)).replace(day=1)

    out = ["BEGIN:VCALENDAR", "VERSION:2.0", "PRODID:-//first-year-bills//EN"]
    out += event("Rork Free credits reset (start of calendar month)",
                 first_of_next, "FREQ=MONTHLY;BYMONTHDAY=1")
    out += event("Rork paid plan renews (anniversary of first purchase)",
                 rork_paid, f"FREQ=MONTHLY;BYMONTHDAY={rork_paid.day}", 2)
    out += event("Apple Developer Program renewal (99 USD)",
                 apple.replace(year=apple.year + 1), "FREQ=YEARLY", 30)
    out += event("Check that free Supabase projects are still active",
                 today + timedelta(days=6), "FREQ=WEEKLY")
    out.append("END:VCALENDAR")
    sys.stdout.write("\r\n".join(out) + "\r\n")

if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

Run it with the `python3` that ships with macOS:

```bash
python3 first_year_bills.py 2026-11-20 2026-12-05 > bills.ics
grep -c BEGIN:VEVENT bills.ics   # should print 4 — one per event
open bills.ics                    # Calendar opens and asks where to import
```

Three choices worth explaining:

- **All-day events.** With times attached, renewals from overseas services can appear to shift by a day across time zones. Dates alone are easier to match against billing emails.
- **Apple alerts 30 days ahead, Rork 2 days ahead.** An annual renewal can need more preparation — an expired card, for one.
- **Supabase weekly, starting in 6 days.** That's one day inside the one-week pause window, so you open the project before it has a chance to sleep.

One caveat: if your first purchase fell on the 29th, 30th or 31st, `BYMONTHDAY` alone will skip the shorter months. Pick a nearby day instead. I couldn't confirm from Rork's docs how its renewal date behaves in those months.

Before, I was in "deal with it when the invoice arrives" mode. After, I open things a day before they'd stop. The second feels noticeably lighter on the stomach.

## Where you're likely to get stuck next

Once the dates are on a calendar, the next question tends to be when to drop the paid plan. Before you do, it's worth confirming that the code you export from Rork can be rebuilt outside it — I walk through that in [checking your exported code rebuilds outside Rork before changing plans](/en/articles/rork-basics/rork-export-rebuild-check-before-plan-change).

If you haven't connected Supabase yet, start with [the two values you need to connect Supabase to Rork](/en/articles/rork-ai/rork-connect-supabase-two-values-publishable-key-project-url); it also shows where to look when a free project's status matters.

It's the items with odd dates, more than the big amounts, that cause the most trouble later. The longer I run apps, the more I find that the payments that come once a year are the ones that slip out of memory.

If you do one thing today, pass your two dates to the script above and get those four events onto your calendar.
