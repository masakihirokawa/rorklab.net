Picture the moment just before you press submit, and a reviewer asks: "What are the rights for this icon?" You had an AI produce dozens of candidates. Which one did you pick? Under which tool's terms was it made? I am honestly not confident I could answer that three months later.

For my ukiyo-e wallpaper app, I keep a note of where every old paper print came from. I do the restoration by hand, but without that provenance record I could not explain later why I am allowed to ship the image. AI-made images, I think, sit in exactly the same place.

I cannot give you a one-line rule for whether an AI-made image is allowed. What I can offer is a practical habit for solo developers: accept what cannot be decided, and **make every image explainable before you submit**. This guide is for anyone about to ship an AI-made icon or illustration who wants to spend half a day on four checks — ledger, terms, resemblance, and dimensions — and come out with a record they can show if review asks.

## What the stores ask for is "made by you, or licensed to you"

App Store Review Guideline 5.2.1 asks you not to use protected third-party material, such as trademarks, copyrighted works, or patented ideas, without permission, and says apps should be submitted by the person or legal entity that owns or has licensed the intellectual property. Section 5.2 opens with the idea that your app should only include content you created or have a license to use.

Nothing there asks whether an image was AI-generated. What matters is whether you can **explain where it came from**. Google Play's policies on intellectual property and impersonation point the same way (both checked on the official pages as of November 2026).

How AI-generated images are treated in law varies by country, and each tool words its terms differently, so I will not draw a legal conclusion here. This guide sticks to four checks.

## Decide first how much to record for each kind of source

Generated images are rarely alone. Most projects mix self-made art, purchased stock, and free assets. Each source needs a different kind of evidence, so I list them up front.

<table>
<thead><tr><th>Source</th><th>What you need to explain it</th><th>Using it as the app icon</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>AI-generated</td><td>Tool name, plan, dated terms, where the prompt is saved</td><td>Only when the terms clearly allow commercial use and you have a resemblance record</td></tr>
<tr><td>Drawn or shot yourself</td><td>Where the source file (layered project or raw photo) lives</td><td>The easiest to explain, so I prefer it for anything that becomes the app's face</td></tr>
<tr><td>Purchased stock</td><td>Receipt and license tier (standard, extended, and so on)</td><td>Check that the license does not forbid use as a logo or trademark</td></tr>
<tr><td>Free or public domain</td><td>Source URL, license name, whether credit is required</td><td>Save a copy of the page in case the source disappears</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

The rule I use: **for the image that becomes your app's face, pick the one whose origin takes the fewest words to explain.** If it takes three lines, it is safer as an in-app illustration.

## Check 1: One ledger row per image

Start by listing every image you plan to ship. No special tool is needed; put one CSV in the project root.

<table>
<thead><tr><th>Column</th><th>What to write</th><th>Example</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>file</td><td>Path relative to the project</td><td>assets/images/icon.png</td></tr>
<tr><td>source</td><td>generated, self-made, purchased, or free</td><td>generated</td></tr>
<tr><td>tool</td><td>Tool name and where the prompt is saved</td><td>Image tool A (prompt in prompts/icon.txt)</td></tr>
<tr><td>date</td><td>Date created (YYYY-MM-DD)</td><td>2026-10-02</td></tr>

<tr><td>similarity_check</td><td>When and how you checked for resemblance</td><td>image search + trademark search 2026-10-03, nothing found</td></tr>

At first I planned to fill it in "once the final pick was settled," and let candidates pile up in one folder. It did not go well. Re-exports mixed with first versions, and I could no longer tell which prompt had produced which file. Now I add the row **the moment I adopt an image**, and not a minute later.

## Check 2: Keep the tool's terms, with a date

Writing "checked" in license_note is not enough, because terms change. Record the date you read them and the heading of the clause you relied on. If you can, save the terms page as a PDF or text file next to the ledger.

Three things to look for:

- Whether generated images may be used in a commercial app
- Whether free and paid plans grant different rights
- Whether the output belongs to you or to the service

If you cannot tell from the terms, it is perfectly reasonable to decide **not to use that tool's output for anything that becomes the app's face**. If you changed plans partway through, note which plan you were on when the image was generated.

## Check 3: Look for resemblance to existing work or trademarks

A generated image can land close to an existing character or logo. The Copycats item in Guideline 4.1 asks you not to ship something that only tweaks a popular app's name or look.

What I do is unglamorous. I run the candidate icon through a reverse image search and see whether anything similar ranks near the top. I search the app name both in the store and in a trademark database (in Japan, J-PlatPat). Finding nothing does not prove you are safe; the value is in **writing down that you searched** in similarity_check.

One more line I hold: if a prompt named an existing brand, artwork, or artist, I drop that image before even checking resemblance. Explaining that something you tried to imitate "does not look similar" is a hard case to make.

## Check 4: Let a script check the submission specs

This part is not judgment; it is what machines are good at. Here is how I summarize the official specs (checked on each official page as of November 2026).

<table>
<thead><tr><th>Asset</th><th>Requirement</th><th>Source</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>iOS icon (source image)</td><td>Square, 1024x1024 as the baseline, no rounded corners or transparent pixels</td><td>Expo docs, App icons</td></tr>
<tr><td>New format for iOS 26 and later</td><td>A .icon file from Icon Composer can be set as ios.icon (since Expo SDK 54)</td><td>Expo SDK 54 changelog</td></tr>
<tr><td>Google Play icon</td><td>32-bit PNG with alpha, 512x512px, up to 1024KB. Play applies the corner radius and shadow, so hand it over as a full square</td><td>Play Console Help; Google Play icon design specifications</td></tr>
<tr><td>Google Play feature graphic</td><td>JPEG or 24-bit PNG without alpha, 1024x500px</td><td>Play Console Help, Add preview assets</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

Stray transparent pixels, or an alpha channel left behind on an image that looks fully opaque, creep in during export and editing. AI tools often hand you transparent PNGs, which makes this an easy place to trip. Here is the script I use alongside the ledger.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Check an image provenance ledger and store submission sizes (v2).

Usage:
  python3 asset_ledger_check.py <project root> [ledger CSV]

Ledger CSV columns (default: assets-ledger.csv):
  file,source,tool,date,license_note,similarity_check
  file is relative to the project root. date is YYYY-MM-DD.
"""
import csv
import re
import sys
from pathlib import Path
from PIL import Image  # pip install pillow

SKIP_DIRS = {"node_modules", ".git", "ios", "android", ".expo", "dist"}
EXTS = {".png", ".jpg", ".jpeg", ".webp"}
REQUIRED = ("source", "tool", "date", "license_note", "similarity_check")
DATE_RE = re.compile(r"^\d{4}-\d{2}-\d{2}$")


def has_transparency(img: Image.Image) -> bool:
    if img.mode in ("RGBA", "LA", "PA"):
        return img.getchannel("A").getextrema()[0] < 255
    return "transparency" in img.info


def has_alpha_channel(img: Image.Image) -> bool:
    # Even when every pixel is opaque, the channel alone can get an upload rejected
    return img.mode in ("RGBA", "LA", "PA") or "transparency" in img.info


def size_notes(rel: str, name: str, w: int, h: int, kb: float, img_fmt: str, alpha_ch: bool, alpha_px: bool):
    notes = []
    in_icon_pkg = any(p.endswith(".icon") for p in rel.split("/")[:-1])
    if in_icon_pkg:
        return notes  # Layers inside an Icon Composer .icon package can be any size
    if "feature" in name:
        if (w, h) != (1024, 500):
            notes.append(f"feature graphic must be 1024x500 ({w}x{h})")
        if alpha_ch:
            notes.append("feature graphic has alpha (use JPEG or 24-bit PNG)")
        return notes
    if "play" in name and "icon" in name:
        if (w, h) != (512, 512):
            notes.append(f"Play icon must be 512x512 ({w}x{h})")
        if img_fmt != "PNG":
            notes.append(f"Play icon must be PNG ({img_fmt})")
        if kb > 1024:
            notes.append(f"Play icon is over 1024KB ({kb:.0f}KB)")
        return notes
    if "icon" in name and not any(k in name for k in ("splash", "adaptive", "notification", "monochrome")):
        if w != h:
            notes.append(f"icon is not square ({w}x{h})")
        elif w != 1024:
            notes.append(f"icon is not 1024x1024 ({w}x{h})")
        if alpha_px:
            notes.append("contains transparent pixels (iOS icons must be opaque)")
        elif alpha_ch:
            notes.append("looks opaque but still has an alpha channel (can trigger ITMS-90717)")
    return notes


def main() -> int:
    if len(sys.argv) < 2:
        print(__doc__)
        return 2
    root = Path(sys.argv[1]).resolve()
    ledger_path = Path(sys.argv[2]) if len(sys.argv) > 2 else root / "assets-ledger.csv"

    ledger = {}
    if ledger_path.exists():
        with ledger_path.open(encoding="utf-8", newline="") as f:
            for row in csv.DictReader(f):
                ledger[(row.get("file") or "").strip()] = row

    problems = 0
    for path in sorted(root.rglob("*")):
        if not path.is_file() or path.suffix.lower() not in EXTS:
            continue
        if SKIP_DIRS & set(path.relative_to(root).parts):
            continue
        rel = path.relative_to(root).as_posix()
        with Image.open(path) as img:
            w, h = img.size
            fmt = img.format or "?"
            alpha_ch = has_alpha_channel(img)
            alpha_px = has_transparency(img)
        kb = path.stat().st_size / 1024
        notes = []

        row = ledger.get(rel)
        if row is None:
            notes.append("no ledger row")
        else:
            for col in REQUIRED:
                if not (row.get(col) or "").strip():
                    notes.append(f"ledger column {col} is empty")
            d = (row.get("date") or "").strip()
            if d and not DATE_RE.match(d):
                notes.append(f"ledger date is not YYYY-MM-DD ({d})")

        notes += size_notes(rel, path.name.lower(), w, h, kb, fmt, alpha_ch, alpha_px)

        status = "CHECK" if notes else "OK"
        problems += bool(notes)
        print(f"[{status}] {rel}  {w}x{h}  {kb:.0f}KB  {fmt}  alpha={'yes' if alpha_ch else 'no'}")
        for n in notes:
            print(f"        - {n}")

    for rel in sorted(ledger):
        if rel and not (root / rel).exists():
            print(f"[CHECK] {rel}  in the ledger but the file is missing")
            problems += 1

    print(f"\nneeds attention: {problems}")
    return 1 if problems else 0


if __name__ == "__main__":
    sys.exit(main())
```

It tells roles apart by file name. Names containing `icon` are treated as the iOS source icon, names containing both `play` and `icon` as the 512px Play icon, and names containing `feature` as the feature graphic. Splash, adaptive, and notification images, plus layers inside an Icon Composer `.icon` package, are skipped for dimension checks (the ledger check still applies).

Here is a run on a test folder, using Python 3.10.12 and Pillow 12.3.0 on Linux (the script is written to behave the same on macOS). I deliberately mixed in `icon-rounded.png` with one transparent corner pixel, `icon-rgba-opaque.png` saved as RGBA though every pixel is opaque, a 1000x1024 `store-icon.png`, and a ledger row with a malformed date.

```text

[CHECK] assets/images/feature-graphic.png  1024x500  3KB  PNG  alpha=yes
        - feature graphic has alpha (use JPEG or 24-bit PNG)
[CHECK] assets/images/icon-rgba-opaque.png  1024x1024  6KB  PNG  alpha=yes
        - looks opaque but still has an alpha channel (can trigger ITMS-90717)
[CHECK] assets/images/icon-rounded.png  1024x1024  6KB  PNG  alpha=yes
        - no ledger row
        - contains transparent pixels (iOS icons must be opaque)
[OK] assets/images/icon.png  1024x1024  5KB  PNG  alpha=no
[CHECK] assets/images/play-icon.png  512x512  2KB  PNG  alpha=yes
        - ledger column similarity_check is empty
        - ledger date is not YYYY-MM-DD (2026/10/05)
[CHECK] assets/images/splash-icon.png  400x400  1KB  PNG  alpha=yes
        - no ledger row

```

`icon-rounded.png` differs by one transparent pixel in the top-left corner. `icon-rgba-opaque.png` has no visible transparency at all. Either can still stop an upload to App Store Connect with ITMS-90717. Those two lines are the whole reason I leave this to a machine. The exit code is 1 when anything needs attention, so you can wire it into a pre-submission step.

## Three common misconceptions

**"AI-generated images can't go on the stores."** In the App Store Review Guidelines and Play policies I read as of November 2026, I found no rule that bans an asset simply for being AI-generated. What is asked is whether you own it or have a license.

**"You'll be rejected unless you declare AI-generated art."** I also found no field asking you to declare this for icons or screenshots. Google Play's AI-generated content policy mainly concerns apps that generate content for users as a feature. That is a different question from how your assets were made, and I try not to mix the two.

**"Pre-rounded corners look better."** Both iOS and Google Play apply the mask themselves, and Google's icon design specifications ask for square assets. Rounding the corners yourself brings two problems at once: transparent pixels and doubled corners.

## What this method cannot tell you

It does not guarantee that no rights problem will arise. The ledger is a record that lets you **explain later what you relied on**. If the terms are hard to read, or the app earns meaningful revenue, a professional is worth consulting.

And the script only looks at dimensions, format, transparency, and blank ledger fields. It cannot tell whether an image resembles existing work — that part still comes down to human eyes and a written record.

## Where you will likely get stuck next

Once the ledger leads you to swap an icon, the next snag is usually "I fixed it, but the old icon is still there" or "I removed alpha and it was rejected again." Most often an old image is still in the build, or the export settings slipped back.

For telling alpha channels apart and removing them, see [It Looks Opaque, but App Store Connect Says the Icon Has an Alpha Channel](/articles/rork-business/app-store-icon-alpha-channel-itms-90717-opaque-looking-png-check-before-upload). If review stops you, the [App Store Rejection Playbook for Rork Apps](/articles/rork-business/rork-app-store-rejection-survival-playbook) covers how to structure a reply. And if you want to inventory everything else you bundle with the same mindset, [your lockfile's dev/prod split won't tell you which licenses your app must credit](/articles/rork-dev/rork-oss-license-inventory-shipped-artifact-boundary).

## If you do only one thing next

For the app you plan to submit, write **one ledger row for the icon**. If your hand stops at the source column, that is exactly the spot to settle before you submit.

**Before asking whether an image is allowed, make sure you can explain it.** I keep that order even on days when I am rushing to ship.
